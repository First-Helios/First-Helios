# validator-v12: calorie counts and carousel positions are no prices (P8-22, ADR-0013 Amendment 36)

Session P8-22, 2026-11-18, under the owner's delegation (2026-11-17: "I trust you to make the
correct decision on the questions"). Found while measuring PDF menus for ADR-0029: Torchy's
gluten-conscious PDF prints "CAL 643" and today's reader takes 643 as a bare price. A check of
`helios_laptop`'s Gold found the same on HTML pages.

Scope:
- **Code:** the shared price reader (`packages/helios_parsing/prices.py`) and the validator's
  version. No model, prompt, chunker, schema or `infra/` change; the page classifier's frozen v4
  reader and the chunker are unchanged.
- **No writes to `helios_laptop` or the Pi.** Gold was measured read-only from the stored texts
  and saved answers. Scripts and outputs stay untracked in the session scratchpad.

## Result

- **The rule:** a bare reading (an unmarked integer or, on a bare page, a one-decimal amount) is
  not taken when a calorie marker comes right before it in its block: "cal 320",
  "Calories: 180", "kcal 450", "Cals: 340", and a range's last number ("cal 400-800",
  "calories: 610 – 920"). Such a number doesn't vote in `bare_page` either. Marked money reads as
  before ("Calories 320 $12.50" still reads $12.50).
- **Second rule, a position counter:** a "k/n" block (k at most n) is not a glass/bottle pair
  when its page also prints "1/n", "2/n" and "3/n" as blocks of their own: a carousel's slide
  numbers (P.F. Chang's menu cards end with "1/4", "2/4", …, "1/26"). A repeated real pair
  ("12/48" on several wines) still reads. Found by the Gold audit in section 7.
- **Gold: 439 false rows, all of them** (143 calorie counts + 296 carousel positions). Of the 2,039 stored texts behind `helios_laptop`'s Menu
  pages, the reader changes 10; re-interpreted from saved answers, two lose accepted prices, and
  everything they lose is a calorie count: Pokeworks (27 accepted rows → 0, e.g. "California Poke
  Bomb $320") and Quiznos (8 → 0, "Chipotle Chicken Zlider $180"). Boston Market and Saxbys print
  calories next to real prices; every accepted price on them is kept. P.F. Chang's `/menu`
  (4 venues) prints no price at all; its 74 accepted rows, every one a slide number ("Asian
  Caesar Salad $5" from "5/7"), go to 0: 296 Gold rows.
  Expected `current_menu` after the apply: **100,448 → 100,009**.
- **Labelled sets: no recall or accuracy loss.** 14 of 2,504 labelled or published texts change,
  every lost token a calorie count or a carousel position; the P8-21 held-out's §8 report and section report are
  identical; the verdict measurement is in section 3.

## 1. Mechanism

`_tokens` reads a block's last integer as a bare price when nothing but punctuation follows it.
A chain's menu often prints calories under each item's name and no price at all (Pokeworks:
"SPICY AHI" / "cal 400-800" / description; Quiznos: "calories: 180"). The model named the item and
copied the calorie number as its price; the validator found that number printed right after the
name and accepted it.

Gold on `helios_laptop` (read-only, every priced row's price Evidence against its block): **143
rows** carry a calorie count as their price, on 6 subjects and 2 page texts (Pokeworks' shared
menu page on 5 venues: 135 rows; Quiznos: 8).

## 6. Not covered

- A calorie marker in another block ("Calories" as its own block above "320") is not covered; no
  Gold row was found in that shape.
- Calories printed after the number ("320 cal") never read as a price: a bare reading needs the
  number to end its block.

## 7. The Gold audit that found the counters

After the calorie rule, every priced Gold row on `helios_laptop` (100,448) was traced to the
block its price was read from (all located: 93,639 marked prices, 6,809 bare). The words right
before the bare ones are dish names, separators ("|", "—", "~", "/", "-") and the two classes
here: calorie markers (161) and "k/n" blocks. Of 440 bare prices read from a "k/n" block, 296
come from one page's position counters (P.F. Chang's `/menu`, `countdiv` blocks after each dish
card); the other 144 are real glass/bottle and size pairs ("8/23", "9/36", "12/48") and keep
reading. No other class of non-price numbers stood out: prices of $150 and more are catering
trays, whole cakes, kegs and wine bottles; prices under $1 are add-ons and sides.

**Found, not fixed here (a page-check question, not a reader one):** retailer catalogs stored
as menus. An Amazon grocery category page is the "menu" of 2 venues (710 Gold rows: real
product prices, but 234 rows are named "Unable to add item", the cart button's text next to each
price), and a Walmart page of 1 venue (14 rows). The page check accepted a product listing; a
fix belongs with the verifier's precision gap (Amendment 35) or with which venues are in scope
(ADR-0028's place types), and is the owner's call.
